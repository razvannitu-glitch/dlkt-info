## Bounded retention checkpoint experiment

When a synthetic candidate reaches 256 terminal attempts, the dedicated archiver may seal exactly one immutable, complete metadata checkpoint under the candidate lock. A new checkpoint is prohibited for open, incomplete, below-capacity, corrupted, or real candidate histories. It contains every attempt, terminal result, associated attestation (including an attested but abandoned attempt), ordered review heads and all scope review records, not only latest successes. Database-local versioned PostgreSQL 17 JSONB/UTF-8/UTC text is bounded to 1 MiB and SHA-256 bound. A separately authenticated reader returns bytes only when their digest and the complete still-retained ledger agree; changed bytes even with a recomputed hash, modified retained history, missing review sequence, and future seal times fail closed. Exact replay does not reseal or change timestamps. The archiver cannot create observations, reviews or read raw tables. A seal preserves capacity-held and denies approval reuse and rollover. It is not a backup independent of the database, a privileged-admin tamper-proof signature, or a restore service. No delete, pruning, baseline reset, epoch rotation or external storage is introduced. Recovery after saturation requires a separately reviewed retention policy, authenticated complete evidence, a new independently reviewed scope baseline and tested restoration controls. Four integration cases exercise ACL, complete 256-attempt export, stable retry and timezone, capacity preservation, rejection and corruption. Production remains excluded.

## Exact attempt recovery (laboratory only)

The strict transaction adapter now shares bounded history readback validation across observation persistence and recovery. A separate authenticated reaper can reconcile one exact attempt only after the locked reader reports expired-open and SQL independently rechecks the deadline under the candidate lock. A lost or ambiguous acknowledgement requires exact abandonment readback, preserving the original attempt, start, attestation and timeout reason. Missing or contradictory history fails closed, an already-terminal attempt is not rewritten, and a newer attempt cannot be abandoned by a stale recovery request. Recovering an attested but unfinished attempt records abandonment, not success. No observation callback, automatic retry, review, archive reset, activation or approval reuse is exposed by recovery. A later observation may start only through the existing explicit committed-start protocol, with every prior failure retained. At capacity, resolving the last open slot produces capacity-held; a checkpoint may then seal all 256 outcomes, but never enables rollover. Seven additional integration cases exercise real separate loopback login, early rejection, stranded starts, attestation loss, ambiguous commit, invalid history, stale target and capacity. The synthetic time fixtures are not real observations. Complete live receipt persistence, independent scope review and safe epoch/retention design remain separate release prerequisites.
